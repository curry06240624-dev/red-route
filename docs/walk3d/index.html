Credits

More in 3D. Walk the route yourself in first or third person with the helmet display (that page shows an AI-generated astronaut model, labelled on screen). Back to the Red Route planner.

Terrain. HiRISE digital terrain model of Jezero Crater, 1 m (USGS Astrogeology, Mars 2020 landing products). Credit: NASA/JPL/University of Arizona/USGS. Shown here on the app's 4 m grid at true vertical scale.

Ground picture. The real orbital photo of the same ground: the HiRISE 25 cm orthomosaic of Jezero (NASA/JPL/University of Arizona, mosaic by USGS Astrogeology), tiles from NASA JPL Mars Trek, stitched to the terrain grid. The photo is black and white; our code gives it a Mars tone.

Route and numbers. The same plan and the same rules as the Red Route app. Stops are places where NASA's Perseverance rover worked (NASA/JPL-Caltech).

Software. three.js r169 (MIT licence). Barlow fonts (SIL Open Font License). Both are stored with this page; nothing loads from another site.

What is drawn, not data. The two crew figures and the base-camp module are simple shapes made by our code. The sky, the sun and the fine ground grain are drawn by our code too. Outside the mapped area (4.7 km by 2.1 km) the ground is a flat placeholder.

No AI-made pictures. The helmet rim in the helmet view is drawn by our code. No AI-made images or models are used on this page.

AI use. The code of this page was written by an AI coding assistant (Claude, by Anthropic), directed by the team.

The clock runs fast: the label next to the play button gives the speed.
